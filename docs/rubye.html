The Aziel project paper · Wife, witness, later litigant

Rubye in the record, and in court against Nadeau

The contemporary report places her in the car. Volume V places later lawsuits on the same name.

The contemporary report in Volume II places Rubye in the car just feet from where Marion’s body landed, then describes her collapse and removal to a hospital. Its account of her experience is narrated by the report rather than presented as a detailed first-person statement from her.

Volume V makes her proximity, subsequent actions, and relationship with the Nadeaus central to its discussion of the human evidence. It states that she later engaged in repeated legal actions against Nadeau over property and estate matters — the same Nadeau Volume IV places in the doorway of the death-scene photograph.

This paper does not add case captions, docket numbers, or court holdings that the volumes do not print. It sets the archive’s own sentences in newspaper columns and points at the reader pages.

Volume V · Chapter 5

Legal actions against Nadeau

As printed in the human-evidence volume. No additional holdings are invented here.

Volume V states that Rubye Nix Zioncheck (later Rubye Nix Wilson) received a significant payoff or offer from Hollywood interests tied to the Nadeau family orbit; that she intentionally flopped the opportunity; and that she later expressed intense hatred of the Nadeaus — language the volume treats as consistent with someone who knew the truth and refused to play along.

Its key facts, as printed: she was close enough to witness key elements of the staging and what happened to Vic; she fled the country, changed her name, and encoded the truth in her artistic work; she received a Hollywood offer or payoff tied to the Nadeau orbit; she deliberately tanked the opportunity; she later engaged in repeated legal actions against Nadeau over property and estate matters; she expressed intense hatred of the Nadeaus.

Volume IV’s doorway chapter already notes the same later lawsuits when it identifies Nadeau as Rubye’s brother-in-law and places him at the center of the moment the official suicide narrative was established.
