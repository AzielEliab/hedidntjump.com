The return west

Safe conduct, then the train

The contemporary account reproduced in Volume II describes Kenneth Romney, the House sergeant-at-arms, arranging safe conduct back to Seattle after Marion’s escape from the Maryland institution. It says Capitol police intervened to prevent another arrest, and that Marion ultimately traveled west by train after an airline refused him passage in Chicago.

Where are records to his “Who’s Crazy Now” speech?

Marion and Rubye

The wife in the car below

Inquiry of the Record · No. 08

Why did his wife never give a statement as the most prolific witness?

The contemporary report in Volume II places Rubye in the car just feet from where Marion’s body landed, then describes her collapse and removal to a hospital. Its account of her experience is narrated by the report rather than presented as a detailed first-person statement from her. Volume V makes her proximity, subsequent actions, and relationship with the Nadeaus central to its discussion of the human evidence.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 09

Why did Rubye hide testimony in her art for a later generation to find?

Volume V states that Rubye fled, changed her name, and “encoded the truth in her artistic work.” Chapter 10, headed Rubye Nix Wilson artistic testimony, transcribes four high-priority works, including “I could not remain silent forever,” “I was with him when it happened. It wasn’t what we were told,” and “THERE IS MORE.” The volume reads those plates as direct statements of presence and of the falsity of the official account, and it recommends multispectral analysis of matching works. It does not print a contemporaneous statement from Rubye that she hid testimony for a later generation; that framing is the open inquiry. The facsimiles sit on the Rubye paper and in Volumes I and V.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 10

Why did Rubye relentlessly sue the Nadeaus after? Was it spite, or was she silenced too?

Volume II preserves the family-battle clippings: “Kin Fight Over Zioncheck Will,” naming Mrs. Ann Nadeau and Mrs. Rubye Nix Zioncheck; “Zioncheck Daughters Fight Over Estate”; “Nurse Defends Zioncheck Will”; and “Zioncheck’s Widow Must Yield Stocks.” Those plates put Rubye and the Nadeaus as opposing names in probate and estate columns. Volume V states that she later engaged in repeated legal actions against Nadeau over property and estate matters, and that she expressed intense hatred of the Nadeaus — language the volume treats as consistent with someone who knew the truth and refused to play along. The same chapter records a Hollywood offer or payoff tied to the Nadeau orbit, which it says she intentionally flopped.

The volumes print the lawsuits and that reading. They do not settle whether the suits were spite, a silenced witness’s only remaining instrument, or both. That tension is the open inquiry. The facsimiles are gathered on the Rubye paper.

Photographs of the record

What a plate can establish

Inquiry of the Record · No. 04

What can photographs establish?

Photographs document visible objects and their positions at the time of exposure. Volume IV examines Nadeau’s position in the doorway and the placement of Marion’s hat and coat on the floor. It compares that arrangement with the account of Nadeau reaching for the hat as Marion moved toward the window.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 05

Why does Nadeau’s account not match the setup of Zioncheck’s office — and why did his account change?

Volume IV records William Nadeau’s official-narrative claims: he stood “one foot away”; Zioncheck “ran around the desk,” “brushed past” him, and “dove headfirst” through the open window in one fluid motion. The same volume measures that sequence against a small, cluttered fifth-floor office — a usable run of 4–6.5 meters, a sash opening of 27–33 inches, and a minimum 2.7–3.8 seconds — and calls the proximity claim physically impossible.

Chapter 8 then sets those statements beside the official death-scene photograph: Nadeau calm in the doorway; hat and coat already arranged on the floor — contradicting the story that he was “reaching for the hat” when Zioncheck jumped. Volume I’s writing account, that Nadeau found Marion writing shortly before the reported jump, belongs in the same frame. The volumes present contradictory statements and a staged-looking plate. They do not print a later signed retraction. Volume II prints the empty office — desk, window, bookcase — under a caption that the farewell message was written there and the desk shown as the congressman left it. Volume IV discusses a WSU doorway plate the supplied PDFs do not reproduce as a standalone image.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 11

Why are the injuries inconsistent with a five-story fall?

Volume IV argues that the described injury distribution fits ground-level restraint and blunt-force trauma more closely than the reported head-first fall. Its comparison focuses on cranial trauma, the neck, the limbs, and the broader pattern of injuries it expects from a fall of that height. This is the injury-pattern argument developed in the physics volume.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 13

Why was the janitor unnamed — and why would he have walked away during the event?

Volume IV’s phantom-janitor chapter states that the official narrative relies on a janitor who allegedly unlocked the office door after the reported jump, and that no janitor matching that role appears in contemporary records with a verifiable name — no Arctic Building employment list, police report, or witness statement supplies a consistent one. The volume treats the locked-door detail as a device that lets the named participants avoid explaining access. Volume V repeats the finding: there was no real janitor matching the described role. The volumes do not record a named man leaving the scene mid-event. Why such a figure would walk away remains open because the archive never identifies him.

The press image

The bogeyman of Washington politics

Inquiry of the Record · No. 23

Was Marion Zioncheck a threat to the establishment that couldn’t be silenced?

Volume V’s prelude chapter rejects the unstable-playboy reading. It calls him a threat to powerful interests who was preparing to expose sensitive operations, and it sets that claim beside third-party plans, G-Men / federal-surveillance interest, a naval-intelligence background, and the Alaska Highway legislation (H.R. 6538). The institutional-sanitization chapter then records House votes in mid-April 1935 (272–0) and February 1936 (~274–0) that expunged his remarks from the Congressional Record, and treats those expungements as a pattern of hostility before the death. Volume I’s opening press sheet — the crop on this page — places J. Edgar Hoover in the contemporary columns beside a “playboy legislator” arrest report; the supplied “Playboy Subdued After Battle” plate is the apartment-row photograph that circulated with that framing. Volume II’s death-day headlines continue it. The bogeyman composite shows him trying on a disguise as the “bogeyman of Washington politics.” The volumes argue he was treated as a threat and silenced before the dinner speech. They do not print an order from every institution named on these plates.

A comparison from the same summer

Chicago, twenty-nine days earlier

Inquiry of the Record · No. 19

Is it a coincidence that Illinois statesman John Bolton died suspiciously less than one month before?

Volume III includes a biographical account of Illinois legislator John M. Bolton’s killing in Chicago on July 9, 1936, following a car chase. That date is 29 days before Marion’s death on August 7. The reproduced material describes contemporary reporting about organized crime, political interests, and competing explanations for Bolton’s killing. The archive places this episode alongside the Zioncheck research as a comparison from the same period.

The closed file

The record that is still requested

Inquiry of the Record · No. 20

Why are most of Nadeau’s records missing? Was he naval intelligence too?

Volume V states that both Macfarlan and Nadeau have almost no records — language it treats as consistent with intelligence ties in a Seattle Navy-intelligence era — and, in the named-participants chapter, that Nadeau’s “intelligence ties (explains lack of records).” The same volume documents Marion’s naval-intelligence background separately. The supplied PDFs do not contain Nadeau service papers, a naval-intelligence file, or a FOIA production on Nadeau. The gap is what the volumes record; they do not prove a naval-intelligence posting. Volume IV’s FOIA recommendations target surveillance files on Zioncheck, not a named Nadeau dossier.

Inquiry of the Record · No. 21

Why are FOIA requests on Marion denied to this day?

The federal-records discussion in Volume IV calls for FOIA and archival requests concerning surveillance or monitoring of Marion during 1935–1936, tied to his institutional conflicts and interest in federal operations. Volume V also calls for access to federal surveillance files. A supplied FBI FOIPA letter dated 31 July 2026 — Request No. 1750194-000 as printed on the plate — closes a Central Records System search: the Bureau states it was unable to identify records on Marion Anthony Zioncheck for the stated 1930–1950 window, and that the request is being closed. Requester identity and agency contact blocks are blacked out on the plate. A no-records closing is not a production of the files the volumes ask for.

The mother’s record

The cause as written

Inquiry of the Record · No. 22

Why was Marion’s mother never informed of his death — and is this why she died of Involutional Melancholia?

The supplied Marion certificate lists the informant as Mrs. Wm. Nadeau, not Frances. Volume II later names Mrs. Frances Zioncheck in the estate columns as the late congressman’s mother. Volume III preserves her own death certificate; the principal cause as written is Involutional Melancholia, with contributory exhaustion / myocarditis as that plate records them. The volumes do not print a telegram, hospital notice, or other instrument showing that she was told of the August 7, 1936 death, and they do not print a statement that she was never told. Whether she was informed, and whether that silence belongs beside the cause as written, remains an open inquiry.
