A labeled reading · The account the morning papers locked

What the official narrative said happened

From the Washington apartment to a fifth-floor window in Seattle. This edition reprints the contemporary reported sequence — not the investigation’s case against it.

This page sets out the official and contemporary press account as the supplied volumes preserve it: a congressman portrayed as unstable, a return to Seattle, and a reported suicide from his fifth-floor Arctic Building office on August 7, 1936.

It is labeled on purpose. The main paper challenges that account. Nothing here is added that the clippings and volume chapters do not print. Quotations stay inside what those plates make readable. Where a headline is the source, the headline is cited.

Washington · The apartment

A midnight scuffle, then the “bad boy” headlines

The August 8 continuation in Volume II, PDF p. 5, is the official-press prelude. It places Marion in a series of Washington episodes: speeding charges, a reputation as the “bad boy of Congress,” marriage to Rubye Louise Nix of Texarkana, a WPA stenographer, and a honeymoon that the same column follows out of the capital and back again.

On the return, the clipping describes a midnight scuffle in the apartment, assault warrants, and a damaged government car. The same column says the couple’s apartment was later reported ruined. The supplied “Playboy Subdued After Battle” plate is the apartment-row photograph that circulated with that story. The names and charges as printed belong to the facsimile. This page does not reconstruct dialogue the plate does not make fully readable in isolation.

That apartment sequence is the official story’s first act: private disorder made public, then used to explain what came next. Open the reader for the column as printed.

Washington · Observation

Gallinger, then Maryland, then a wall

Volume II’s same column says Marion was taken to Gallinger Hospital for mental observation, and that he was later confined at a Maryland institution from which the report describes an escape. Volume I preserves the observation photographs that circulated with that story — including the plate headed “Too Much Work or Was It Too Much Play?”

In the official sequence, the hospitals are the explanation for the later return west and for the image the August 8 report says he meant to rebut. The clippings are the source. This edition does not reconstruct clinical findings the plates do not print.

The return west

Safe conduct, Chicago, the train

The official-press return is the account already boxed on the main paper as Inquiry 01. Kenneth Romney, House sergeant-at-arms, is described as arranging safe conduct back to Seattle after the Maryland escape. Capitol police, the column says, intervened to prevent another arrest. An airline in Chicago refused passage; Marion traveled west by train. The report connects the return with his mother in Seattle and Romney’s assistance.

Volume II, PDF p. 4, then places him back in the congressional race. The August 8 report says he re-entered to show the public he was neither crazy nor foolish. That sentence is the official story’s motive for staying in the spotlight: a public rebuttal, not a planned speech the investigation later asks after.

Seattle · August 7, 1936

Hatless, coatless, a fifth-floor window

The August 7 Associated Press lead preserved in Volume II, PDF p. 4, is the death paragraph the official narrative never left. Representative Marion A. Zioncheck, it says, fell hatless and coatless from a fifth-floor window of his campaign headquarters in the Arctic Building. The body landed a few feet from where his bride, Rubye Louise Nix, sat waiting in an automobile. She collapsed and was taken to a hospital. The same plate reports that the coroner said all indications pointed to suicide, and that an inquest was set.

Volume I, PDF p. 18, is the companion official-account clipping: “Congressman Zioncheck Is Suicide Friday Evening.” The writing account boxed on the main paper sits here too: Nadeau found Marion writing shortly before the reported jump. Volume IV later restates Nadeau’s claims as the official office sequence — one foot away; ran around the desk; brushed past; dove headfirst through the open window in one fluid motion.

Volume II, PDF p. 3, prints the empty office the press captioned as the room in which a farewell message was written, the desk shown as the congressman left it. The photographed note on House stationery is Volume II, PDF p. 13.

That is the locked story: an unstable return, a sudden jump, a note, a wife in the car below. The main paper keeps the questions that reading will not close.
